Checked on 1 November 2026. Each row of the question sheet¹ was compared with its page's own
words, read in context and not only the quoted excerpt.

- **Result:** 133 yes, 3 no, 3 can't confirm, out of 139 rows.

## For you: the six rows that need you

What the pages say differently (a new version should put these right):

- **Q75, new Tuition Fee Loan, "the amounts may change".** The page says this only about the
  £39,160 total, not every amount.
- **Q95, new Maintenance Loan, "the amounts may change".** The page says this about the
  Tuition Fee Loan total, not the Maintenance Loan. Nothing on the page says the £15,415
  maintenance amount may change.
- **Q104, apprenticeship funding, "the employer pays 5%".** That's only for an employer that
  doesn't pay the apprenticeship levy. An employer that pays the levy, and has used up its
  levy funds, pays 25%.

What the pages don't settle:

- **Q101, apprenticeship funding, "the government pays the provider directly".** The page
  says so only for the 95% share. It doesn't say who gets the money when the government
  pays all of it, for an apprentice aged 16 to 24.
- **Q110, T Level start date "not stated".** No provider or next start date is given. But the
  page does mention past September starts, and a list of providers offering courses from
  September 2025. Is "not stated" fair? Yes keeps the row as it is.
- **Q115, the nursing degree's UCAS date.** On the page, "13 Jan" is the date for "Equal
  consideration date for applications for most undergraduate courses", in the 2027 entry
  section. The page shows no year beside it and doesn't name nursing. Is a nursing degree
  one of "most undergraduate courses", and is this 13 January 2027? Yes lets Careerwright
  use 13 January 2027 as the date to apply by.

Two more things for you, not rows:

- UCAS isn't a GOV.UK page. Whether Careerwright may keep words from it is still yours to
  decide. This check didn't decide it.
- The sheet says the new student finance page "names none" of the courses it covers. Its
  eligibility part does give examples, starting with "a bachelor’s degree". It also names
  nursing among courses that can get extra Tuition Fee Loan ("priority additional
  entitlement"). That may help you answer Q115.
